## New

**Cursor shaders.** ZenTerm now ships two GLSL effects that draw over the terminal surface and follow the cursor. Cursor Warp is a smear in the style of Neovide. Cursor Tail is a lighter comet. Pick one in Settings → Terminal → Cursor shader and it applies to every open pane without a restart, or set `cursor-shader = cursor_warp` (or `cursor_tail`) in `~/.config/zen-term/config`. Off is the default, and one at a time: the two shaders are the whole list, so nothing untested can reach your GPU. They show up on cursor moves, which means they read best in an editor like neovim. Scrolling is untouched.

## Fixed

**The terminal follows the display it is on.** A window dragged to a display with a different pixel density kept rendering the terminal at the old density, and macOS scaled the result, so text came out soft. It now re-renders at the new display's density on the move. Vsync moved with it: a pane used to stay pinned to the refresh rate of the display it was born on, so a 60Hz pane dragged to a 120Hz display stayed at 60.

## Install

ZenTerm updates itself. On v0.2.0 or later, the update card appears in the app. For a first install, open the DMG and drag ZenTerm to Applications. macOS 14 or later, Apple Silicon.

## Reporting a bug

From inside the app: Help → Report an Issue prefills the version and your environment. You can still open an [issue](https://github.com/praxis-labs-io/zen-term/issues) directly.
